When a convolutional network is trained end to end through a variational quantum circuit
(VQC) classifier head, the head shapes the features the network learns, and we measure this
with one common linear probe on the frozen bottleneck of each tuned network. On CIFAR-10
with 4,000 training images, an 8-qubit VQC head with CNOT rings lowers probe accuracy by 4.7
points relative to a linear head (5 seeds, every seed lower; a two-hidden-layer MLP probe finds the
same gap), and removing the rings recovers 3.8 of those points. An exact identity yields a predictor of which layouts cost most: with every variational rotation
at zero, each readout is a product of cosines of the encoded inputs over a set fixed by the
CNOT layers, and probe accuracy falls as the mean order of these products rises (73.6 at
order 1, 69.8 at the default 2.5, 60.1 at 5.25). Classical heads trained on the same products follow the trend, and entangling gates alone do not set the cost: a CZ ring of order 1 probes 0.4 points below the ring-free head, and multi-qubit readouts without entangling gates fall on the trend.
The association holds, with a few rank reversals and varying size, for six new CNOT layouts, six layouts under a second encoding, twice the training budget, MNIST, SVHN and an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 (default head 6--8 points below linear at approximately matched updates, 1,000--10,000 images; ring-free within 1.4), and in 10-seed means under data re-uploading and on a pretrained MobileNetV3.
For independent inputs, high-order products that keep their magnitude drive the mean mass of their inputs in the graded middle of the encoding toward zero as order grows; the bound is loose at the tested orders and not shown to cause the loss. A pre-registered
intervention that removes saturation, with the forward pass unchanged, recovers half of
the highest-order cost, by a route we do not identify. Without entangling gates and with single-qubit readouts the head is exactly a classical per-coordinate sinusoidal model, and under matched per-head tuning a linear head has the highest mean test
accuracy among linear, small-MLP and default VQC heads in all six matched dataset--size settings.
