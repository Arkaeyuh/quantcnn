When a convolutional network is trained end to end through a variational quantum circuit
(VQC) classifier head, the head shapes the features the network learns, and we measure this
with one common linear probe on the frozen bottleneck of each tuned network. On CIFAR-10
with 4,000 training images, an 8-qubit VQC head with CNOT rings lowers probe accuracy by 4.7
points relative to a linear head (5 seeds, every seed lower; a two-layer MLP probe finds the
same gap), and removing the rings recovers 3.8 of those points. An exact identity organizes the pattern: with every variational rotation
at zero, each readout is a product of cosines of the encoded inputs over a set fixed by the
CNOT layers. Probe accuracy falls as the mean order of these products rises, from 73.6 at
order 1 to 69.8 at the default order 2.5 and 60.1 at order 5.25; classical heads trained on
the same products follow the same trend, the circuit ordering holds at twice the training
budget and on MNIST, six new CNOT layouts show a strong inverse
association with order (two rank reversals), as do six layouts under a second encoding, and a circuit that retains entangling gates but has product order 1 recovers 3.1 points. On an
ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 the same ordering holds: at an approximately matched number of optimizer updates
the default head costs 6--8 points with 1,000 to 10,000 training images, while the ring-free
head stays within 1.4 points of a linear head; the ordering also holds on SVHN, with data re-uploading and on a pretrained MobileNetV3. Entangling gates alone do not set the size of the cost here: an entangling CZ ring of order 1 probes 0.4 points below the ring-free head, and multi-qubit readouts of a circuit without entangling gates fall on the order trend. We prove that, for independent inputs, high-order products that keep their magnitude drive the mean mass of their inputs in the graded middle of the encoding toward zero as order grows, and a pre-registered
intervention that lets saturated coordinates escape, with the forward pass unchanged, recovers half of
the highest-order cost. Without entangling gates and with single-qubit readouts the head is exactly a classical per-coordinate sinusoidal model, and under matched per-head tuning a linear head has the highest mean test
accuracy among linear, small-MLP and default VQC heads in all six matched dataset--size settings.
