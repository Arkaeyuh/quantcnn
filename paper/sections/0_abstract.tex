When a convolutional network is trained end to end through a variational quantum circuit
(VQC) classifier head, the head shapes the features the network learns, and we measure this
with one common linear probe on the frozen bottleneck of every trained network. On CIFAR-10
with 4,000 training images, an 8-qubit VQC head with CNOT rings lowers probe accuracy by 4.7
points relative to a linear head (5 seeds, every seed lower), and removing the rings recovers
3.8 of those points. An exact identity organizes the pattern: with every variational rotation
at zero, each readout is a product of cosines of the encoded inputs over a set fixed by the
CNOT layers. Probe accuracy falls as the mean order of these products rises, from 73.6 at
order 1 to 69.8 at the default order 2.5 and 60.1 at order 5.25; classical heads trained on
the same products follow the same trend, the circuit ordering holds at twice the training
budget and on MNIST, designed classical supports favor lower order at fixed mean overlap
between readouts, and an entangling circuit with product order 1 recovers 3.1 points. Without CNOTs the head is exactly a classical per-coordinate
sinusoidal model, and under matched per-head tuning a linear head has the highest mean test
accuracy among linear, small-MLP and default VQC heads in every setting we test.
