\section{Tuning Changes Head Comparisons, but a Linear Head Stays Ahead}
\label{sec:tuning}

\input{figures/table1_matched_tuning}

Before turning to representations, we check how much a head comparison depends on the
training recipe. In an exploratory sweep with one shared recipe (head learning rate
$10^{-3}$ for every head, 25 MNIST epochs; \cref{app:sweep}), the VQC beat MLP-8 on MNIST by
17.8, 18.0 and 6.9 points at 250, 500 and 1,000 images and lost to it on CIFAR-10 by 14.1,
13.8 and 8.3 points. Under the protocol of \cref{sec:setup} (50 MNIST epochs,
validation-based selection, per-head learning rates), these differences change in both size
and sign (\cref{tab:matched}). On MNIST, VQC $-$ MLP-8 becomes $-2.1\pm9.7$, $+3.3\pm2.6$
and $+0.8\pm1.0$. Part of this change comes from the protocol itself: at the shared rate
under the new protocol the advantage is already $+10.8$, $+6.1$ and $-0.5$ (last column of
\cref{tab:matched}), and per-head tuning moves it to the values above. On CIFAR-10, where
only the learning rate changes between the two columns of \cref{tab:matched}, the VQC deficit to MLP-8 shrinks from 10.1--13.6 points to
3.1--9.2, and the VQC's own test accuracy rises by 6.0, 6.2 and 10.7 points. A linear head
has the highest mean in all six settings and beats the VQC for every seed, by 1.7--13.7
points on MNIST and 7.0--15.8 on CIFAR-10. Its selected rate sits at the top of its grid in
five settings, so its lead may be understated.

This audit fixes the comparisons used in the rest of the paper: every head is evaluated at
its own validated learning rate. It also leaves a question. At 4,000 CIFAR-10 images the
VQC trails the linear head by $7.0\pm1.2$ test points (5 seeds: $6.7\pm1.0$). Part of that
gap could come from the VQC as a readout and part from the backbone it trains; a common probe
measures the gap that remains under one readout.
