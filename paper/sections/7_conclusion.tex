\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A quantum classifier head trained jointly with a CNN changes the representation the CNN learns:
under one common probe, the default VQC head lowers CIFAR-10 probe accuracy by 4.7
points. Across CNOT layouts, encodings, a CZ ring and multi-qubit readouts the cost tracks product
order, as predicted in advance, and entangling gates are neither sufficient nor necessary for it.
High-order products confine their inputs to the ends of the encoding, where the bottleneck saturates
early and stays; letting saturated coordinates escape recovers half of the highest-order cost. In practice: tune heads separately, probe the backbone, count product order, and keep the bottleneck out of saturation.
