\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A quantum classifier head trained jointly with a CNN changes the representation the CNN learns:
under one common probe, the default ring-entangled VQC head lowers CIFAR-10 probe accuracy by 4.7 points, and the drop tracks
the product order of the head, with predictions recorded in advance: across CNOT layouts, encodings,
a CZ ring and multi-qubit readouts, order rather than entanglement carries the cost. High-order
products must push their inputs out of the middle of the encoding; the bottleneck saturates early and
stays saturated, and letting saturated coordinates escape recovers half of the highest-order cost. In
practice: tune each head separately, probe the backbone, count a circuit's product order before
training, and keep the bottleneck out of saturation.
