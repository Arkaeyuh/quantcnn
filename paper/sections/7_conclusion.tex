\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A quantum classifier head trained jointly with a CNN changes the representation the CNN
learns. With every head tuned on validation data and every backbone read by the same linear
probe, the default ring-entangled VQC head lowers CIFAR-10 probe accuracy by 4.7 points, and
the drop follows a quantity fixed by the CNOT pattern: the order of the cosine products each
readout computes at zero rotations. Lowering that order, by removing the rings or by
entangling only in pairs that cancel at zero rotations, recovers most of the gap, and classical heads built from the same products reproduce the
declining probe-accuracy trend. The pattern holds on a pretrained ResNet-18, where at a matched number of
updates the default head costs 6--7 points at every training size we test. Without CNOTs the quantum head is a
classical sinusoidal model. For practitioners, three steps follow: tune each head
separately, probe the backbone, and count the product order of a circuit before training
through it.
