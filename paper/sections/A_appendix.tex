\section{The Zero-Rotation Identity}
\label{app:identity}

Let $U(\theta)$ be the variational block and $\ket{\psi(\vz)}=\bigotimes_j R_y(\enc{j})\ket{0}$
the encoded product state, so that $\ZZ{k}=\bra{\psi}U^\dagger Z_k U\ket{\psi}$. At
$\theta=0$ every $R_y$ and $R_z$ is the identity and $U$ is the product of the circuit's
CNOTs. Conjugation by $\mathrm{CNOT}(c,t)$ maps $Z_t\mapsto Z_cZ_t$ and fixes $Z_c$, so
$U^\dagger Z_k U$ is obtained by applying these rules to $Z_k$ for the gates in reverse
order; the result is a string $\prod_{j\in\Sk}Z_j$, with $\Sk$ the set of qubits on which an
odd number of propagation paths land. Since $\ket{\psi}$ is a product state with
$\bra{\psi}Z_j\ket{\psi}=\cos\enc{j}$, the expectation factorizes into
\cref{eq:identity}. \Cref{tab:supports} lists the supports. Pairs after every layer apply
each pair CNOT four times, which is the identity, so their supports equal the no-CNOT ones;
with trained rotations between the layers the gates no longer cancel (its outputs differ
from the ring-free circuit's at random rotation angles), and the circuit can entangle: with
every input at zero and every rotation zero except $R_y(\pi/2)$ on qubit 0 in the last layer,
the final pair CNOT turns qubits 0 and 1 into a Bell pair, whose single-qubit reduced state
has purity $1/2$.

For the ring-free circuit and any rotation angles, qubit $j$ starts from Bloch vector
$(\sin\enc{j},0,\cos\enc{j})$ after encoding and then undergoes a fixed rotation $M_j\in
\mathrm{SO}(3)$ determined by its trained $R_yR_z$ layers. Hence
$\ZZ{j}=(M_j)_{zx}\sin\enc{j}+(M_j)_{zz}\cos\enc{j}$, which is \cref{eq:ringfree} with
$\beta_j=(M_j)_{zx}$ and $\alpha_j=(M_j)_{zz}$. The test suite checks the formula against the simulator (tolerance
$10^{-10}$). Since the readout layer is linear, the ring-free head is
$W[\alpha_j\cos\enc{j}+\beta_j\sin\enc{j}]_j+b$.

\section{Gradient Attenuation at Zero Rotations}
\label{app:attenuation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:atten}
At zero rotations, for $j\in\Sk$ and $\enc{i}=\pi\tanh z_i$,
\begin{equation*}
  \Big|\frac{\partial \ZZ{k}}{\partial z_j}\Big| \le \pi\prod_{i\in\Sk\setminus\{j\}}|\cos\enc{i}| .
\end{equation*}
If, in addition, the coordinates $z_i$, $i\in\Sk$, are independent, then
\begin{equation*}
  \mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\frac{\partial \ZZ{k}}{\partial z_j}\Big)^2\Big]
  \le \pi^2\prod_{i\in\Sk\setminus\{j\}}\mathbb{E}\big[\cos^2\enc{i}\big]
  \le \pi^2\rho^{|\Sk|-1},\qquad \rho=\max_{i\in\Sk}\mathbb{E}\big[\cos^2\enc{i}\big]\le1 .
\end{equation*}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Differentiating \cref{eq:identity} gives
$|\partial\ZZ{k}/\partial z_j|=\pi\,\mathrm{sech}^2(z_j)\,|\sin\enc{j}|\prod_{i\in\Sk\setminus\{j\}}|\cos\enc{i}|$,
and $\mathrm{sech}^2\le1$, $|\sin|\le1$. Under independence the expectation of the squared product
factorizes, and $\mathbb{E}[\pi^2\mathrm{sech}^4(z_j)\sin^2\enc{j}]\le\pi^2$.
\end{proof}

Under independence, each additional input in a readout's support therefore multiplies the bound
on the mean squared gradient that every other input receives through that readout by
$\mathbb{E}[\cos^2\enc{i}]$, which is below 1 unless $\enc{i}$ concentrates at $0$ or $\pm\pi$; if
$\rho=1$ the bound need not fall. The proposition bounds the size of the
per-readout gradient; it does not by itself show that the backbone learns a worse representation,
and an input can still receive gradient through other readouts. On the trained bottlenecks
(CIFAR-10, 4,000 images, seeds 0--2, validation embeddings), $\mathbb{E}[\cos^2\enc{j}]$ ranges from
0.37 to 1.0 across coordinates (mean 0.63 for the default circuit, 0.72 for the last-ring
circuit), and the measured $\mathbb{E}[\prod_{i}\cos^2\enc{i}]$ follows the independence product
closely: 0.39 vs 0.39 with two other factors for the default circuit, and 0.11 vs 0.09 with
seven for the last-ring circuit, a factor of roughly 0.6--0.8 per additional input. Coordinates
with $\mathbb{E}[\cos^2\enc{j}]$ near 1 sit at $\enc{j}\approx\pm\pi$, where $\tanh$ saturates: they leave
the other inputs' gradients unattenuated but receive almost none themselves.

supports if attenuation drives the cost, and committed a training guard and a refutation
criterion. On the default supports training stays within the guard and there is no observed mean

exclude the predicted 2 points), which meets that support's part of the refutation
criterion; on the last-ring supports $\tau=1$ lowers clean training accuracy by 11 points, beyond
the guard, so that support is inconclusive and the joint criterion is not met; $\tau=0.3$ has no
reliable effect. We expected a partial recovery and found none. The attenuation hypothesis is
therefore not supported: in this test, removing the attenuation of the other factors did not
recover the representation. The test does not exclude attenuation acting otherwise, for instance
inside trained circuits or through each input's own derivative; \cref{prop:atten} remains a
correct bound on gradient size, not a demonstrated mechanism.

\section{An Encoding Whose Zero-Rotation Readouts Vanish}

With the phase encoding, $H$ then $R_z(\pi\tanh z_j)$, each qubit's Bloch vector is

\section{Bottleneck Saturation}
\label{app:saturation}

\Cref{tab:geometry,tab:saturation} test where the cost arises, in three pre-registered steps. First,
on the trained backbones of 38 configurations (both layout families, the default and two alternative
encodings, both widths and the classical product heads), we measured the fraction of saturated bottleneck
coordinates, the effective dimensionality of the bottleneck and that of its class means, and committed in
advance that a candidate must correlate with probe accuracy at $\rho\le-0.7$ (saturation) or
$\rho\ge0.7$ (dimensionality) over the VQC and over the classical cells. All three pass. We had expected
saturation to track the probe poorly across encodings, and it did not. A secondary criterion also
passes: within the 13 8-qubit layouts, the part of effective dimensionality not explained by order
tracks the part of probe accuracy not explained by order ($\rho=0.87$; criterion $\ge0.5$). Saturation
and dimensionality are themselves tightly coupled ($\rho=-0.91$ across cells), so the diagnostics alone
cannot order them.

Second, in a discovery cohort (seeds 0--4), we added one penalty to the loss of the classical product
heads, either against saturation or against correlated bottleneck coordinates, with the weight selected
on validation accuracy. Both pass their manipulation checks (last-ring saturation $\le0.42$ under the
first: 0.03; an effective-dimensionality increase of at least 0.05 under the second: 0.28 to 0.93), and
both improve fit. On the default products the anti-saturation penalty meets every criterion and
decorrelation does not; on the last-ring products both raise the probe substantially
(\cref{tab:saturation}). We had expected the anti-saturation penalty to fail; the decorrelation pattern,
larger on the last-ring than on the default products, was as expected, its default gain below the
criterion. The MLP probe agrees (anti-saturation $+0.5$, $+2.0$, $+12.2$; decorrelation $-0.4$, $+0.0$,
$+8.0$ on the order-1, 2.5 and 5.25 heads). At the non-selected weights (3 seeds), anti-saturation gives
$+0.6$, $+2.9$ and $+10.7$ points, and decorrelation $-3.0$, $-0.3$ and $+3.7$.

Third, the same anti-saturation penalty on the 8-qubit circuits passes its manipulation check
(saturation 0.30 to 0.002 and 0.49 to 0.005, against thresholds of 0.18 and 0.30), improves fit, and
raises probe accuracy on the default and last-ring circuits, positive in every seed under the linear
probe, as predicted, with a small gain on the ring-free circuit ($+0.6\pm0.8$, within the specificity
criterion); the MLP probe gives $+1.6$, $+7.7$ and $+0.4$ against its extended-grid baselines (one negative default seed). The gains recover
about 44\% and 59\% of the two circuits' gaps to the unregularized ring-free head; the rest remains
unexplained, and the refutation condition (gains below $+1$ on both) is not met.

Finally, a replication cohort (seeds 5--9). The training guard we committed, clean training accuracy
within 10 points of the unregularized head, was meant to exclude lost fit but as written also excluded
the large gains in fit on the last-ring heads (13.6 to 23 points), which left those results
inconclusive. We therefore re-ran both arms of the last-ring comparison on fresh seeds, with the penalty
fixed at 10 and a guard committed as no loss of fit. The replication meets every criterion: $+6.0\pm4.1$

probe gives $+6.1$ and $+9.4$. The penalty acts during training only, so the heads keep the same forward
architecture. Under criteria as committed, the results support a causal role for saturation on both
supports and both head families; the penalty also changes fit and effective dimensionality, so
saturation need not be the only route.

\section{The Signal Budget}
\label{app:budget}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:budget}]
Write $c_j=\cos\enc{j}$ and $t_j=|\tanh z_j|$. Independence gives
$\mathbb{E}|r_k|=\prod_{j\in\Sk}\mathbb{E}|c_j|\ge a$, so the largest $\mathbb{E}|c_j|$ is at least
$a^{1/m}$. On the middle zone $\delta<t_j<1-\delta$ we have $|c_j|=|\cos\pi t_j|\le\cos\pi\delta$,
and $|c_j|\le1$ everywhere, so
$\mathbb{E}|c_j|\le1-(1-\cos\pi\delta)\Pr(\delta<t_j<1-\delta)$. Rearranging gives the bound. With
identically distributed factors every $\mathbb{E}|c_j|$ equals the largest.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Without independence.} Since $|c_i|\le1$, $|r_k|\le|c_j|$ for every $j\in\Sk$, so
$\mathbb{E}|c_j|\ge a$ and the bound holds for every input with $a$ in place of $a^{1/m}$; this
version needs no assumption on the joint distribution but loses the dependence on order. Some
assumption is needed for that dependence: if all inputs of a support are equal, $r_k=c^m$ and
$\mathbb{E}|c|^m\ge a$ allows a middle-zone mass up to $(1-a)/(1-\cos^m\pi\delta)$, which tends to
$1-a$ rather than to 0 as $m$ grows. (Generalized H\"older,
$\mathbb{E}\prod_j|c_j|\le\prod_j(\mathbb{E}|c_j|^m)^{1/m}$, gives this bound for some $j$ under any
dependence.) The stated order dependence therefore assumes independence; its robustness to dependence is not established. On the trained bottlenecks, the measured $\mathbb{E}[\prod_i\cos^2\enc{i}]$ matches the independence
product to within 0.04 in the moments measured (\cref{app:attenuation}), which is consistent with, but does not establish, near-independence.

The proposition says the middle zone must empty as order grows; it does not say at which end the
coordinates gather. At $z\approx0$ a coordinate gives $c=+1$, and at saturation $c=-1$ with
$\mathrm{sech}^2z\approx0$. Near either end a coordinate's factor is locally insensitive to it, so graded variation in a high-order support has little effect on the readout. Averaged over a support, the bound gives $\frac1m\sum_j\Pr(\text{middle})\le-\log a/\big(m(1-\cos\pi\delta)\big)$ under independence, so the mean middle-zone mass vanishes as $m$ grows, though a single coordinate need not leave it. The same argument
holds for any encoding whose factors are bounded by 1 and are flat only at the ends, including $\enc{j}=z_j$, with the zones defined around the extrema of $\cos z$ at multiples of $\pi$.

\begin{proposition}[Flat ends]\label{prop:flat}
Let $t=|\tanh z|$ and $g(z)=\big|\tfrac{d}{dz}\cos(\pi\tanh z)\big|$. Then $g(z)\le\pi^2t$,
$g(z)\le2\pi^2(1-t)^2\le8\pi^2e^{-4|z|}$, and $g(z)>\pi\delta\sin\pi\delta$ if $\delta<t<1-\delta$.
Under the assumptions of \cref{prop:budget} with identically distributed factors,
$\Pr\big(|\partial r_k/\partial z_j|>\pi^2\delta\big)\le(1-a^{1/m})/(1-\cos\pi\delta)$ for every $j\in\Sk$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:flat}]
$\cos(\pi\tanh z)$ is even in $z$, so take $z\ge0$; then $g(z)=\pi(1-t^2)\sin\pi t$. Near zero,
$\sin\pi t\le\pi t$ and $1-t^2\le1$, so $g\le\pi^2t$. Near saturation, $\sin\pi t=\sin\pi(1-t)\le\pi(1-t)$ and
$1-t^2=(1-t)(1+t)\le2(1-t)$, so $g\le2\pi^2(1-t)^2$; and $1-t=2/(e^{2z}+1)\le2e^{-2z}$ gives
$g\le8\pi^2e^{-4z}$. On $\delta<t<1-\delta$, concavity and symmetry of $\sin\pi t$ on $[0,1]$ give
$\sin\pi t\ge\sin\pi\delta$, and $1-t^2>1-(1-\delta)^2=\delta(2-\delta)>\delta$. For the last claim,
$t\le\delta$ gives $g\le\pi^2\delta$ and $t\ge1-\delta$ gives $g\le2\pi^2\delta^2\le\pi^2\delta$ since $\delta<\tfrac12$,
so $g(z_j)>\pi^2\delta$ only on the middle zone. By \cref{eq:grad} and $|\cos\enc{i}|\le1$,
$|\partial r_k/\partial z_j|\le g(z_j)$, and \cref{prop:budget} bounds the middle-zone probability.
\end{proof}
Two further facts: $1-t\ge e^{-2z}$ for $z\ge0$ gives $\mathrm{sech}^2z=(1-t)(1+t)\ge e^{-2z}$,
and $1-\cos x\le x^2/2$ with $\cos\pi t=-\cos\pi(1-t)$ gives $|\cos\enc{j}+1|\le\tfrac{\pi^2}{2}(1-t)^2\le2\pi^2e^{-4z}$.
The flatness at saturation thus has two sources, $\mathrm{sech}^2z$ and $\sin\enc{j}$, each decaying as
$e^{-2|z|}$. The escape-only estimator of \cref{sec:backbone} removes only the first, so for a cosine-product readout a saturated coordinate's inward gradient still
decays as $e^{-2|z|}$, the rate of a plain $\tanh$ bottleneck, rather than not at all. This holds for the zero-rotation products only: a trained
circuit readout can contain a component $\beta_j\sin\enc{j}$, whose surrogate inward gradient tends to $\pi|\beta_j|$ rather than to zero. With the non-saturating encoding $\enc{j}=z_j$, $g(z)=|\sin z|$ has no flat tail,
only isolated zeros at multiples of $\pi$. The bounds concern the readout's local sensitivity, not the loss.
A coordinate that the budget pushes to either end carries little gradient through its own readouts, but
the bounds do not show that gradient descent cannot move it there or out again.

\paragraph{Where the trained coordinates sit.} On the test embeddings of the archived heads (CIFAR-10,
4,000 images, seeds 0--4), the middle-zone fraction $M$ and the mean factor magnitude
$\mathbb{E}|\cos\enc{j}|$ are 0.84 and 0.61 without CNOTs, 0.63 and 0.73 for the default circuit, and
0.46 and 0.82 for the last-ring circuit; for the default and last-ring products, 0.53 and 0.78, and 0.35
and 0.86. The near-zero fraction falls with order (0.15, 0.08, 0.05), so the mass leaves the middle
for saturation.

\paragraph{A toy model.} A 32--64--8 MLP on a ten-class Gaussian mixture, trained through classical
product heads on the paper's supports (5 seeds), reproduces the falling middle zone and a probe cost
that generally rises with order (with reversals at orders 3 and 4), but a smaller one (3.7 points from order 1 to 5.25) and with coordinates pinned
near $z\approx0$ rather than at saturation. Tripling the backbone's initial output scale moves the pinning to saturation without enlarging the default support's cost against order 1 ($-2.4\pm1.0$ at the original scale, $-2.3\pm0.7$ at three times it). The toy is consistent with an order-associated cost without predominant saturation; in the CNNs, which are saturated by epoch 5, the interventions of \cref{app:dynamics} support an additional role for saturation.

\section{Training Dynamics and Interventions on the Saturation Trap}
\label{app:dynamics}

Dynamics and intervention runs: CIFAR-10, 4,000 images, small CNN, learning rate $10^{-2}$ (the selected rate of the
unregularized heads), 80 epochs. Predictions were committed before each batch of runs.

\paragraph{Dynamics.} We retrained the ring-free, default and last-ring circuits (seeds 0--2) with a
checkpoint every 5 epochs. These runs reproduce the archived per-seed probes exactly. At each
checkpoint we fit the common linear probe and measured $S$ and $M$ (\cref{tab:dynamics}). Every head
saturates early, and the heads then differ in how far they desaturate. We had pre-registered two
timing hypotheses that assumed saturation would rise during training (saturation's half-way rise
preceding the probe gap's, or the middle zone's drop preceding saturation's). Saturation is past its half-way point at the first checkpoint (epoch 5) in every run, while the probe gaps reach half their final size at epoch 5 or 10. The first hypothesis, stated as ``no later than'', is therefore met as written in all three seeds, but only because saturation is already high at the first checkpoint, so it does not establish that saturation comes first; the second cannot be scored at this resolution and is inconclusive.
We then pre-registered a follow-up with a checkpoint after each of epochs 1--10 (same runs, which reproduce
the 5-epoch checkpoints): the last-ring head's saturation should exceed the ring-free head's by at
least 0.10 no later than the epoch at which the probe gap first reaches half its maximum, in at least 2
of 3 seeds. It holds in all three: the saturation gap is already 0.18--0.32 after epoch 1, while the
probe gap first reaches half its maximum at epochs 8, 2 and 9. Seed-mean saturation peaks after one or
two epochs, at 0.55 (ring-free), 0.78 (default) and 0.82 (last ring). The probe
gap peaks at epoch 15 (default) and 35 (last ring) and then partly closes as $S$ slowly falls, consistent with an incomplete escape under this training budget; this agrees with the doubled-budget results of \cref{sec:controls}.

\paragraph{Straight-through estimators.} A plain straight-through estimator replaces
$\mathrm{d}\tanh z/\mathrm{d}z$ by 1 in the backward pass and leaves the forward pass unchanged. On the
classical product heads it raised the last-ring probe by $5.7$ points and lowered the default products'
by $1.0$. On every circuit it diverged (head-mean validation accuracy 18--25\%, backbone gradient norm up to $2.4\times10^4$): a sinusoidal component $\beta_j\sin\enc{j}$ of a circuit readout keeps a gradient of order $\pi\beta_j$ at saturation while its forward value is flat, which can drive $z_j$ further out with only an exponentially small change in the loss; we take this as the likely cause.
Under the committed training guard these cells are inconclusive. After seeing this we pre-registered
the escape-only estimator, which uses $\mathrm{d}\tanh z/\mathrm{d}z=1$ only when the descent step
moves $z$ toward 0 and $\mathrm{sech}^2z$ otherwise, per sample and coordinate. Its manipulation check
(saturation down by at least 30\%) and training guard (clean training accuracy no more than 10 points
below baseline) pass in every cell; clean training accuracy rises. The committed hypothesis required
paired gains of at least $+1.5$ (default) and $+3$ (last ring), each positive in at least four of five
seeds, and a ring-free gain below half the last-ring gain. The observed $+1.48$, $+6.83$ and $+0.89$
(5 of 5 seeds positive in each) miss the first threshold by 0.02; the refutation condition (gains below
$+1$ on both circuits) is not met. We had expected weaker results (a default gain of 0 to $+1.5$ and
saturation falling by less than 30\% on the default circuit).
Because the default gain missed by so little, we pre-registered a replication on fresh seeds 5--9 with the same design and criteria
(the unregularized last-ring arm is the retest arm on the same seeds); the seeds 0--4 outcome stands as scored.
The replication meets every criterion: paired gains of $+3.30\pm3.02$ on the default circuit
($68.3\to71.6$), $+5.56\pm5.58$ on the last ring ($60.0\to65.6$), each positive in 5 of 5 seeds, and
$+1.31\pm0.40$ without CNOTs, below half the last-ring gain. Seed-mean saturation falls to at most $0.0006$ in
every escape-only head, and clean training accuracy rises in every cell. The MLP probe gives $+2.54$ (default) and $+1.30$ (no CNOT).
Pooled over the ten seeds, the gains are $+2.39$, $+6.19$ and $+1.10$. We had expected a default gain of about $+1.5\pm1$.

\paragraph{Induced saturation.} To manipulate saturation in the other direction, we added to the
ring-free circuit's loss a penalty $\lambda\,\mathrm{mean}_{j<k}\,\mathrm{relu}(0.9025-\tanh^2z_j)$ that
pushes the first $k\in\{2,4\}$ coordinates into saturation ($\lambda=10$, 5 seeds). The manipulation
check passes ($S=0.40$ and $0.53$), and the probe falls to $59.8\pm22.2$ and $61.5\pm10.1$, which
meets the committed criterion for saturation alone accounting for most of the cost at matched $S$. We
do not count it, because no training guard was committed for this test and the penalty damaged fit:
clean training accuracy fell to 71.5\% and 72.9\% (96.6\% without the penalty), and one $k=2$ seed never
trained (head at chance, probe 20.5\%). Without that seed (an exclusion we did not commit) the $k=2$ drop
is 3.8 points at $S=0.27$, close to the ring-family cost of 3.6 interpolated linearly in $S$ to that value, and clean training accuracy is
86.7\%. The test as run cannot separate saturation from lost fit.

\paragraph{Generality runs.} The CZ, readout and $\enc{j}=z_j$ heads were tuned like every new head
(learning rates $\{3\times10^{-3},10^{-2},3\times10^{-2}\}$ on seeds 0--2, seeds 3--4 at the rate
selected on validation); every head selected $10^{-2}$, the middle of the grid. The MLP probe agrees:
the $\enc{j}=z_j$ slope is $-2.0$ points per unit order under it ($-2.1$ under the linear probe).
Under $\enc{j}=z_j$ the identity of \cref{eq:identity} holds with $\cos z_j$; its fraction ``$S$'' is
not comparable to the $\pi\tanh$ heads', because nothing bounds the scale of $z$.

\section{Effective Order Beyond Zero Rotations}
\label{app:efforder}

\input{figures/tableA_effective_order}

Product order is defined at zero rotations. \Cref{tab:efforder} extends it to any rotations with a nonzero readout by
expanding each readout operator in Pauli strings and counting, with weight $c_P^2$, the inputs
that each string visible on the encoded state touches; the test suite checks that the expansion
reproduces the simulator's readouts and input dependences exactly, and that it equals
\cref{eq:identity} at $\theta=0$. Three observations follow. First, layouts with a single
CNOT layer after the last rotations cannot change their effective order at any rotations where the readout is nonzero,
because single-qubit rotations do not change which qubits a string acts on; only every-layer
layouts can move, and of these brickwork moves most, from 3.5 to 2.6 at its trained rotations,
below the distance-2 ring's 3.0, which restores that pair's probe ranking and shrinks its
residual from the fit from $+5.1$ to $+2.3$ points. Second, at random rotations
$\theta\sim\mathcal{U}(-\pi,\pi)$ the default ring's readouts depend on all eight inputs
(effective order 5.4) while the ring-free ones stay at one, consistent with the gap at large
initialization (\cref{tab:init}). Third, random-rotation order predicts probe accuracy worse than
order at $\theta=0$ (Spearman $-0.71$ vs $-0.91$): the star and the open chain reach 3.4 and 4.2
at random rotations yet probe near order-1 circuits, because trained circuits stay close to their
zero-rotation regime. Order computed at the trained rotations ranks the circuits slightly better
($-0.94$), but it uses the trained weights and is therefore a post hoc description, not a
prediction.

\section{SVHN, Data Re-uploading and a Second Pretrained Backbone}
\label{app:extensions}

Three further tests had predictions and pass criteria committed before their additional runs (\cref{tab:extensions}); as committed, the second SVHN test reuses the first test's runs wherever a head's selected rate is unchanged (20 of its 60 scored runs).
Each head's learning rate was selected on the mean validation accuracy of seeds 0--2, with one half-decade

probe; the MLP probe changes no verdict.

\paragraph{SVHN.} A first test (5 seeds) left the no-CNOT and pairs heads at the lower edge of their grids,
so its order comparisons were inconclusive; its gaps were driven by runs that collapsed to the majority
class (head validation accuracy below 25\%), in four of five last-ring seeds. A second, separately
committed test extended the grids downward and used 10 seeds. At the newly selected rates no run
collapses, and the order holds seed by seed: default $-$ no CNOT $-12.8\pm3.8$ and last ring $-$ default
$-18.1\pm9.5$, every seed negative, while the pairs circuit, which entangles at order 1, probes

for no CNOT) fails, because there are none. The classical products head is $1.6\pm9.2$ below the default
circuit, within the committed two points.

zero-rotation identity no longer holds, and we predicted only that the single-encoding ordering would
carry over. It does: default $-$ no CNOT $-10.0\pm3.2$ and last ring $-$ default $-9.7\pm7.1$ (every seed

last layer $-4.9$, default $-11.8$, last ring $-11.8$), the two ring layouts about twice as much, and
saturation again rises with order.

direction repeats but the cost is much smaller. The pairs circuit is $1.5\pm0.6$ above the default and the linear head $1.6\pm0.8$ above it, every seed, and
pairs is $0.6\pm0.6$ above the ring-free head (4 of 5 seeds; pass).
The order test fails on one part: default $-$ no CNOT is $-0.9\pm1.0$, negative in every seed but short of
the committed $-1.0$, while last ring $-$ default is $-5.5\pm5.8$ (every seed). The default circuit
saturates little on this backbone ($S=0.13$, against 0.30--0.49 for ring circuits on the small CNN).

\section{Learning-Rate Sensitivity}
\label{app:lr}

Trigonometric heads, quantum or classical, tolerate a narrow range of head learning rates under
the coupled recipe (backbone at half the head rate). On CIFAR-10/4,000 all of them select
$10^{-2}$, and at $3\times10^{-2}$ test accuracy falls to 63.4 for the default VQC, 50.0 for
the ring-free VQC, and 29.3 and 28.9 for the classical cos and cos+sin heads, while the linear
head selects $3\times10^{-2}$. With the head learning rate fixed, the default VQC also
collapses at backbone learning rates of $3\times10^{-2}$ and $6\times10^{-2}$, where its
bottleneck saturates the encoding almost completely, whereas the linear head still trains
(\cref{tab:controls}). Test accuracy for every grid point is in the released results.

\section{Sign Symmetry}
\label{app:sign}

At zero rotations every readout is an even function of each $z_j$, because cosine is even.
We measured how far trained heads are from this symmetry on CIFAR-10/4,000 test embeddings:
the median, over samples, of $\|q(-\vz)-q(\vz)\|/\|q(\vz)-q(\vz')\|$, with $q$ the readouts
and $\vz'$ a random other sample (0: blind to the sign; about 1: flipping the sign moves the
readout as far as changing the image). The frozen circuit stays nearly sign-blind
(0.09--0.12), and its accuracy on $-\vz$ is within 1.4 points of that on $\vz$. Trained heads
are not: 0.58--0.73 for the default VQC (accuracy on $-\vz$: 25--34\%), 0.91--1.02 for the
ring-free VQC (6--17\%), and 0.85--0.97 for the monotone and symmetric encodings of
\cref{tab:controls}. Sign-blindness at initialization therefore does not predict the
backbone cost, consistent with the null result of the monotone encoding.

\section{Training and Reproducibility Details}
\label{app:repro}

\paragraph{Training.}
AdamW with weight decay $10^{-4}$, cosine annealing to zero, batch size 32, gradient clipping
at norm 1.0 (MNIST) or 2.0 (CIFAR-10); CIFAR-10 augmentation is random crop, horizontal flip,
small rotation and color jitter. Head learning-rate grids: MNIST
$\{3\times10^{-4},\dots,3\times10^{-2}\}$ and CIFAR-10 $\{10^{-3},3\times10^{-3},10^{-2}\}$ in
half-decade steps, each extended by one step whenever the selected rate was at the top; circuit
variants $\{3\times10^{-3},10^{-2},3\times10^{-2}\}$; classical product heads
$\{10^{-3},\dots,3\times10^{-2}\}$ on CIFAR-10 circuit supports and
$\{3\times10^{-3},\dots,3\times10^{-2}\}$ on designed supports and on MNIST (extended to
$10^{-1}$ for the MNIST last-ring products). All selected rates of circuit and classical product heads
are interior to their grids, except in the re-tuned matched-budget search on the pretrained
backbone (one half-decade step either way), where the 10,000-image default head and the linear
head at both sizes select a boundary rate; that search is local. The probe uses AdamW (weight decay $10^{-4}$), batch size 128,
60 epochs with a cosine schedule, and probe learning rates
$\{3\times10^{-3},10^{-2},3\times10^{-2},10^{-1},3\times10^{-1},1\}$, all selected on
validation.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
The repository contains per-epoch metrics and metadata for every training run, the probe, frozen-head and
trained-circuit measurements, and the scripts that regenerate every number, table and figure
from them (\texttt{scripts/summarize\_v2.py}, \texttt{scripts/zero\_angle\_check.py},
\texttt{paper/figures/scripts/}). The PyTorch statevector simulator is tested against
PennyLane (tolerance $10^{-6}$ in outputs and input gradients, $10^{-5}$ in rotation
gradients); the test suite also checks the zero-rotation identity, the ring-free form, the
entangling example of \cref{app:identity}, the equality of the zero-rotation circuit and the
products head, and the effective-order expansion of \cref{app:efforder} against the simulator.
The predicted orderings for the MNIST replication and the predictions and pass criteria for the
second family of layouts were written in the project's log before those runs; the log entries are
copied verbatim into the repository, whose first timestamp for them postdates the runs, so the earlier dates are our own report. The
predictions for the two encoding arms (\cref{app:encoding,app:phase}) were committed before their runs. Seeds control
data subsets, initialization and data order. Experiments ran on one Apple M3 Max. The reviews
and the claim and citation audits of this paper were run with the ARIS workflow
\citep{yang2026aris}.
